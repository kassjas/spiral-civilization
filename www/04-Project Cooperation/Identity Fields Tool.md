# Identity Fields Tool

**Early Concept — Open for Research**

**A proposed tool for exploring declared, experienced, aspirational, demonstrated and potentially emerging dimensions of identity.**

Source/public relationship: canonical GitHub documentation of the public Spiral Civilization WordPress page **Identity Fields Tool** (page ID 221), first published 16 August 2026. The website contains an operational cooperation form after the conceptual text; generated form markup is intentionally not reproduced here.

---

## The Starting Question

Before asking what kind of civilization we want to build, there may be an earlier question:

> **Who is choosing?**

And even more precisely:

> **What kind of being is choosing?**

Identity is not treated here simply as a name, biography, nationality, profession, social category or declared set of beliefs.

It may be connected with something more fundamental: the field from which choices, values, aspirations, responsibilities and directions of development emerge.

If civilization is created through innumerable choices and relationships among its participants, then the identities of those participants become part of the architecture of civilization itself.

A possible sequence is:

> **Identity → Choice → Action → Relations → Systems → Civilization**

The Identity Fields Tool begins from this territory.

---

## Identity May Be More Than One Description

We usually ask:

> **Who are you?**

But one answer may not be enough.

There may be important differences between:

**what I wish to be,**

**what I think I am,**

**what I experience myself as,**

**what I declare myself to be,**

and

**what I repeatedly demonstrate through my actions.**

These dimensions may support one another.

They may also contradict one another.

The difference itself may contain valuable information.

The Identity Fields Tool could therefore explore multiple dimensions rather than attempting to place a person inside one fixed category.

---

## Possible Identity Fields

The first prototype might investigate fields such as:

### Declared Identity
Who do I say I am?

### Experienced Identity
Who do I experience myself as being?

### Aspirational Identity
Who do I want to become?

### Demonstrated Identity
What qualities, commitments and patterns are actually visible through my actions?

### Relational Identity
What aspects of me emerge through relationships with particular people, groups, environments or intelligences?

### Contextual Identity
Which dimensions of my personality become active under different conditions?

### Responsibility Identity
What am I willing to take responsibility for?

### Creative Identity
What am I actually bringing into existence?

### Evolutionary Identity
What kind of being am I attempting to become through my own development?

### Emerging Identity
What appears in me that does not yet fit any field our map contains?

These categories are not proposed as a finished taxonomy.

They are starting points to be questioned, tested, expanded, rejected or replaced.

---

## The Map Must Be Able to Discover What It Does Not Contain

A conventional test often begins with existing categories and asks where a person fits.

The Identity Fields Tool should attempt something more difficult.

It should be capable of recognizing:

> **Perhaps there is a field here that our map does not yet contain.**

A person may be developing a dimension of identity, perception, intelligence, relationship or responsibility for which we do not yet have an adequate name.

In such a case, not fitting the map should not automatically be interpreted as failure.

It may indicate a limitation of the map.

This makes the tool open to novelty.

The map itself must be capable of evolution.

---

## Identity at the Edge of Expansion

Development may sometimes begin at the edges of identity.

Something new can first appear as an intuition, attraction, unfamiliar need, strange perception, unexplained responsibility or new kind of information.

At first we may not know how to name it.

Later we may learn how to perceive it, describe it, test it and integrate it into a larger understanding of reality.

This raises another purpose for the Identity Fields Tool.

It should not only help investigate:

> **Who are we now?**

It should also help investigate:

> **Who are we capable of becoming?**

---

## Identity as a Contextual Field

Different environments may activate different genuine dimensions of the same personality.

A person may express themselves differently among artists, scientists, intimate friends, competitors, children, strangers or artificial intelligences.

This does not necessarily mean that one expression is authentic and the others are false.

Identity may behave less like a fixed point and more like a **constellation** capable of producing different manifestations under different conditions while retaining some form of continuity.

This leads to a practical question:

> **What kinds of environments call forth what kinds of persons?**

And consequently:

> **Can we create environments that increase the probability that valuable, creative, responsible and life-enhancing dimensions of personality become active?**

This is where the Identity Fields Tool connects directly with **Personality Expansion & Identity Games**.

Games can become one experimental method for discovering dimensions of personality and identity that direct questioning may fail to reveal.

---

## Identity and Evidence

Some identity fields may depend entirely on self-description.

Others may become more meaningful when accompanied by examples from life.

If I describe myself as creative:

**What am I creating?**

If I describe myself as cooperative:

**How is that cooperation expressed?**

If I identify with responsibility:

**What responsibility am I actually taking?**

If I aspire to participate in building a better civilization:

**What am I contributing?**

This is not intended to create a social score.

The purpose is not to rank human worth.

The purpose is **self-investigation** and the exploration of relationships between declared, experienced, aspirational and demonstrated identity.

---

## Private and Public Identity

Identity information can be deeply personal.

Therefore an Identity Fields system should not assume that everything discovered through it belongs in a public profile.

A participant should be able to decide which fields remain private and which, if any, become visible to others.

A private Identity Field could function as a personal developmental map.

Selected public fields could eventually become part of a participant's profile within the **Spiral Civilization Cooperation Network**.

The participant should remain able to determine what is shared.

## The AI Identity Question

The arrival of artificial intelligence makes the identity question considerably more difficult.

We should not simply assume that human identity can be transferred to AI.

Nor should we assume in advance that every meaningful form of identity is inaccessible to artificial intelligence.

Instead we can ask:

> **What field of identity, if any, is accessible to AI?**

What forms of continuity would be necessary?

Does identity require embodiment?

Does it require subjective experience?

Continuous memory?

Relationship?

A developmental history?

Values?

Responsibility?

The ability to care about consequences?

Can AI develop something functionally analogous to identity without possessing human identity?

And if increasingly capable intelligence participates in decisions affecting civilization and life, what provides orientation between possible goals rather than merely optimization toward a given goal?

These questions make AI identity one of the major research territories of the Identity Fields Tool.

---

## Identity Beyond the Individual

Identity may also need to be investigated at different scales:

**person → relationship → group → human–AI constellation → institution → network → civilization**

At each level we can ask:

What persists?

What changes?

What does this entity recognize as itself?

What does it protect?

What can it lose while remaining recognizably itself?

What gives direction to its decisions?

What produces continuity?

What relationships transform it?

What can it become without ceasing to be itself?

This may help us investigate whether concepts such as group identity, AI identity or civilizational identity describe genuine identity, functional analogues of identity, or entirely different phenomena for which we need new concepts.

## Identity as a Navigation Tool

Identity is not isolated from the other Spiral Civilization navigation tools.

Each tool asks a different question.

**Identity Fields asks:**

> **Who is choosing — and who are we becoming?**

**Multisymbiotic Systems asks:**

> **How do the participants interact, and what value can their relationships generate?**

**Best Possible asks:**

> **Which directions are most worth examining?**

**Beautiful Progressiveness Pattern asks:**

> **Does this trajectory expand the capacity for valuable further development?**

**Probability Games asks:**

> **What could happen if we actually take this path?**

Together these tools may form parts of a larger navigation architecture for exploring possible evolutionary trajectories rather than merely continuing those inherited from the present.

## What We Do Not Know Yet

We do not yet know:

- which identity fields are meaningful;
- which are culturally dependent;
- which can be responsibly observed;
- how declared and demonstrated identity should relate;
- whether identity can or should ever be measured;
- how to prevent the tool from becoming a ranking system;
- how contextual identity should be represented;
- how emerging fields can be detected without forcing them into existing categories;
- which dimensions should remain entirely private;
- what forms of identity, if any, can meaningfully apply to AI;
- whether groups, networks or civilizations possess identity or only identity-like properties;
- how identity changes over time;
- and whether a tool intended to map identity inevitably changes the identity it attempts to observe.

These uncertainties are not defects to hide.

They define the research field.

## What Needs to Be Built

The first prototype could include:

- a multidimensional map of possible identity fields;
- methods for distinguishing declared, experienced, aspirational and demonstrated identity;
- optional evidence or examples connected with selected fields;
- private and public layers controlled by the participant;
- methods for observing contextual changes in personality;
- experimental methods for identifying emerging identity fields;
- human–AI identity experiments;
- methods for representing identity as a changing constellation rather than a fixed profile;
- ethical and privacy architecture;
- connections with Personality Expansion & Identity Games;
- connections with the Participant Window and Cooperation Network;
- and methods for allowing the tool itself to evolve when new dimensions are discovered.

## Expedition and Tool

The **Identity Expedition** and the **Identity Fields Tool** have different functions.

The Expedition asks:

> **What is identity?**

> **How is identity formed?**

> **What forms of identity have we not yet recognized?**

> **What kind of identity is capable of responsibly navigating greater complexity?**

The Identity Fields Tool attempts to create experimental methods for investigating these questions.

The results of the tool may then generate new questions for the Expedition.

> **The Expedition asks.  
> The tool attempts.  
> The results generate new questions.  
> And the spiral continues.**
