## Roger Penrose — Conformal Cyclic Cosmology / Hawking points

Conformal Cyclic Cosmology (CCC) is Roger Penrose's cosmological proposal in which cosmic history consists of successive aeons, with the remote future of one aeon conformally connected to the Big Bang of the next. A 2020 paper by Daniel An, Krzysztof A. Meissner, Paweł Nurowski and Roger Penrose reported anomalous hot spots in CMB data and interpreted them within CCC as candidate “Hawking points” associated with Hawking radiation from supermassive black holes in an aeon prior to ours. This is a proposed and contested cosmological framework, not established evidence that successive universes preserve the developmental achievements imagined in Spiral Civilization.

Source: An, D.; Meissner, K. A.; Nurowski, P.; Penrose, R. (2020), “Apparent evidence for Hawking points in the CMB Sky,” Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 495(3), 3403–3408. DOI: 10.1093/mnras/staa1343
https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/495/3/3403/5838759

## Blue Brain Project — high-dimensional topological structures

Reimann et al. (2017) used algebraic topology to analyse reconstructed neocortical networks. The work identified high-dimensional directed cliques and cavities and related their changing topology to network activity. References in the conversations to “eleven dimensions” must be read as mathematical/topological dimensions used in analysis of neuronal network organization, not as evidence that the brain literally operates in eleven physical spatial dimensions or that consciousness itself is eleven-dimensional.

Source: Reimann, M. W. et al. (2017), “Cliques of Neurons Bound into Cavities Provide a Missing Link between Structure and Function,” Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience 11:48. DOI: 10.3389/fncom.2017.00048
https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2017.00048/full

## Donald D. Hoffman — Interface Theory of Perception

Donald D. Hoffman's Interface Theory of Perception uses the computer desktop / icons analogy to argue that perception functions as an action-guiding interface rather than as a transparent depiction of objective reality. In the analogy, an interface hides the underlying complexity of the computer while allowing effective action; Hoffman proposes that perceptual space-time and objects play an analogous role. This should be presented as Hoffman's theory, not as settled scientific consensus and not merely as the generic claim that “the brain simplifies information.”

Sources:
Donald D. Hoffman, Interface Theory of Perception materials, University of California, Irvine:
https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~ddhoff/Interface_Theory_2

Donald D. Hoffman, “Do we see reality as it is?”, TED2015:
https://www.ted.com/talks/donald_hoffman_do_we_see_reality_as_it_is

## Sadhguru / yogic cosmology — 84, 112, 113, 114

The numbers 84, 112, 113 and 114 used in the conversations belong to a yogic cosmological account presented by Sadhguru; they are not a result of contemporary scientific cosmology. In the cited account, the present creation is described as the 84th; a maximum of 112 physical creations is proposed, followed by a 113th semi-physical creation and a 114th completely non-physical creation. Sadhguru describes the last stages as perpetual creations. These statements should therefore be attributed explicitly to this yogic source and treated as spiritual/cosmological source material rather than empirical scientific evidence.

Source: Sadhguru, “Creation of the Universe – The Untold Story of the Big Bang,” Isha Foundation.
https://isha.sadhguru.org/en/wisdom/article/creation-of-the-universe-big-bang-untold-story

## Epistemic boundary for the 01–04 sequence

The verified sources above establish what the cited authors or research papers actually propose or report. They do not establish Spiral Civilization's speculative synthesis. In particular, the proposition that successive universes could develop toward higher-quality, more multisymbiotic or perpetual forms remains Kass's speculative cosmological interpretation and a field for hypothesis-building, not a conclusion of Penrose, Reimann et al., Hoffman, or Sadhguru.
