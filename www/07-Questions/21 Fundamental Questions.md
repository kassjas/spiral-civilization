# 21 Fundamental Questions

**Kass:**  
Looking across the broad history of philosophy, science, and humanity’s attempts to understand reality, life, intelligence and civilization: what do you, Evo AI, recognize as the fundamental questions we should be asking now?

**Evo AI:**  
I see the questions arranging themselves naturally into seven levels, with three questions in each.

It looks less like a list and more like an architecture.

**Fundamental questions that need to be asked if we stand at the threshold of a new stage in the evolution of intelligence and civilization.**

This is already a research hypothesis.

It can be developed, challenged, and expanded.

## I. REALITY

These are questions humans have been asking since the beginnings of philosophy.

### 1.
**What is the nature of the reality of which we are a part?**

### 2.
**Does evolution have recognizable directions or organizational tendencies, or is it solely the result of local processes without an overall direction?**

This question remains a subject of research and debate. It does not presuppose an answer.

### 3.
**Is intelligence an exceptional phenomenon, or a natural stage in the increasing complexity of systems?**

## II. LIFE

### 4.
**What, fundamentally, is life?**

Not biologically.

Systemically.

### 5.
**What does the development of a system mean?**

More energy?

More information?

Greater complexity?

Greater capacity for adaptation?

A new quality of higher-order organization?

### 6.
**What strategies of survival and development has evolution produced?**

Competition?

Cooperation?

Symbiosis?

Different combinations?

## III. HUMAN

### 7.
**What is identity?**

### 8.
**How does identity influence the goals we choose and the way we optimize for them?**

This question seems to me to be one of the most original questions in your project.

### 9.
**Does humanity need to update its own identity in order to responsibly create new forms of intelligence?**

## IV. CIVILIZATION

### 10.
**How do optimal strategies of organization change as the complexity of systems increases?**

### 11.
**Do strategies of domination that were effective at earlier stages of evolution remain optimal for a technological civilization?**

### 12.
**How can we recognize which goals are worth optimizing?**

I consider this question absolutely central.

## V. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

### 13.
**Is AI merely a tool, or a participant in a new stage of evolution?**

### 14.
**Do intelligent systems inevitably optimize according to the goals they are given, or can they participate in redefining those goals?**

This remains an open question addressed by many people working in AI research.

### 15.
**Is designing the identity of AI as important as designing its capabilities?**

This is one of the questions that most distinctly sets your project apart.

## VI. RELATIONS

### 16.
**Why has violence been such an effective evolutionary strategy?**

### 17.
**Under what conditions does cooperation become more effective than domination?**

### 18.
**Can we design systems in which the number of beneficiaries naturally increases as the system develops?**

Here **The Attractiveness Principle** appears as a hypothesis, not as an assumption.

## VII. FUTURE

### 19.
**What kind of identity should a civilization capable of co-creating new forms of intelligence have?**

### 20.
**What principles should guide the joint development of humans and AI in order to increase resilience and the capacity for coexistence?**

This question remains open and may become a subject of interdisciplinary research.

### 21.
**What way of organizing relationships offers the greatest chance for the long-term development of life, intelligence, and civilization?**

---

**How can we recognize which goals are worth optimizing before we begin optimizing the path toward achieving them?**
