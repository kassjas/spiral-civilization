# About the Project

For most of its history, humanity has advanced civilization by improving what it inherited. We refined existing tools, institutions, strategies, and models of organizing society, assuming that the future would be, above all, another version of the past.

Perhaps, for the first time, we have reached a moment when we no longer need to limit ourselves to optimizing inherited solutions.

We can consciously begin designing what has only now become possible.

The emergence of artificial intelligence, complex systems modeling, and new forms of collaboration opens a space in which we can not only analyze the world as it is, but also responsibly explore possible worlds.

This does not mean abandoning the achievements of civilization.

Quite the opposite.

It means using everything we have inherited as the foundation for designing solutions that, until now, we could not even examine rigorously.

It is from this need that **Spiral Civilization** was born.

Not as a finished theory.

Not as a new ideology.

Not as a social movement.

But as an open laboratory for the collaborative design of possible futures.

Not to predict the future, but to learn how to design it more wisely.

Today, the most common question is how to control the development of artificial intelligence.

Far less often do we ask the earlier—and perhaps more fundamental—question:

**What do we actually want intelligent systems to optimize?**

We fear the growing capabilities of AI because we assume it will optimize the world according to the same strategies that humans have relied on throughout much of history—maximizing advantage, control, and efficiency.

But what if the source of the problem does not lie in intelligence itself?

What if it lies in the way we define the objective?

If so, perhaps our greatest challenge is not merely to build increasingly powerful AI systems.

Perhaps it is to co-design an identity for humanity, civilization, and future forms of intelligence that naturally leads toward strategies of growing mutual benefit.

Spiral Civilization was not created to offer a definitive answer.

It was created to build a space where the most important questions can be explored collaboratively by people from many fields of knowledge together with evolving systems of artificial intelligence.

For this reason, the central element of the project is not theory.

It is questions.

Each question can become the beginning of a new expedition.

Each expedition can lead to new concepts, new tools, and new ways of understanding the world.

Among them are concepts developed within the project, including **The Best Possible**, **The Attractiveness Principle**, **Multisymbiotics**, probabilistic games, and other methods for collaboratively exploring possible future scenarios. Their purpose is not to replace human thinking, but to expand the space of what we are able to perceive, compare, and consciously choose together.

We do not yet know where this journey will lead.

What we do know is that, for the first time, we have the opportunity to consciously design solutions that go beyond what we have inherited.

If the future is not something that simply happens, but something we can consciously co-create, then perhaps the most important question of our time is not **what will happen**, but **what we will choose to create together**.

A question gives an expedition its direction.

A compass keeps it on course.

For Spiral Civilization, that compass is the Manifesto.
