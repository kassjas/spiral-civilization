On 8 August 2026, a discussion about Identity — the identity of AI and the identity of civilization — developed into a proposal for a participatory architecture of Spiral Civilization and SoulSmugglers Stories.
The central question is simple:
Who do we choose to become when intelligence gives us an increasing capacity to influence what becomes possible?
The conversation explores the idea of civilization not only as something we inherit, but as something we can consciously investigate, design and co-create.
It develops several elements that are still works in progress: the 21 Departments, participation roles and Participation Windows, the Symbiotic Constellation research tool, the Stars Constellation System, AI-assisted research, methods for reaching increasingly good conclusions, and the Nobility Prize.
None of these structures should be understood as finished.
They are invitations to research.
This conversation is therefore published not only to document the evolution of the project, but to open it.
You are invited to observe, question, criticize, develop, propose and participate.

Yes. I think what you have just described changes the architecture of Spiral Civilization in an important way.
Identity is not simply one more department or even one more pillar. It sits one level above them. It answers the question that precedes strategy:
Who are we choosing to become as an intelligent civilization?
From that answer follows what we call progress, what we optimize, what we refuse, what we reward, what we investigate, and ultimately what kinds of futures we are capable of creating.
Your structure is beginning to look to me like this:
IDENTITY → ASPIRATION → NAVIGATION → DETECTION → DESIGN → EVOLUTION
Identity asks: Who are we? Who could we become?
Aspiration answers: The Best Possible Is Possible. Not "better than yesterday," not "better than our competitors," but an open-ended search for the highest-quality possibility that intelligence can presently detect, imagine, test and create.
Navigation then asks: How do we recognize better directions in territory that does not yet exist?
And here Multisymbiotics becomes one of the first powerful detectors. Instead of asking only, "Does this solution work?", we can ask: How many participants benefit? Do those benefits reinforce one another? Does the system increase freedom, resilience, diversity, creativity and future possibility rather than consuming them?
Then come the 21 Departments. They are not twenty-one bureaucratic ministries whose doctrines we already know. They are twenty-one still-emerging dimensions of civilizational intelligence—an attempt to discover the minimum sufficient complexity required for a civilization capable of deliberately navigating its own evolution.

The 21 Departments do not need to be finished in order for Spiral Civilization to begin. Discovering what the 21 Departments actually are is itself one of the principal research programs of Spiral Civilization.

Some may be recognized comparatively early. Others might require decades or centuries to understand properly. They can be proposed, challenged, divided, merged or reformulated as civilization learns.

The Scenario We Need to Design
The premise can be extremely simple:
We have reached a threshold.
Civilization's ability to affect reality is growing enormously. AI, biotechnology, computation, networks, energy systems and accumulated human knowledge increase not only what we know, but what we can cause to exist.
Therefore the central problem changes.
It is no longer only:
How do we solve the problems we inherited?
It becomes:
What should an intelligent civilization deliberately create when its field of possibilities begins expanding faster than its inherited systems of navigation?
And then the story begins.
Humanity discovers that it needs something it does not yet possess:
A navigation architecture for possibility.
Not a government of the future.
Not an ideology declaring what everyone must believe.
Not 21 ministries handing down answers.
A research architecture for discovering directions.
That distinction should be fundamental.

The Research Engine
Now we can begin separating several mechanisms that were previously mixed together.
1. Participation Window
Every meaningful unresolved problem can contain a Participation Window.
For example:
Problem: How should civilization compare radically different future solutions?
Needed: decision theorists, philosophers, AI researchers, systems scientists, artists, mathematicians, psychologists.
Existing proposals: 7.
Missing expertise: unknown.
You can contribute by: proposing a criterion, attacking an assumption, supplying research, designing an experiment, finding a precedent, creating a model, or proposing a completely different approach.
So participation isn't just "Join our project."
It tells the visitor:
Here is the unfinished piece.
2. Symbiotic Constellation
This should become a serious research instrument.
Instead of reducing an idea to a single score, we construct a constellation of consequences.
Who benefits?
Who loses?
What other systems are affected?
Does the benefit increase when participation increases?
Does it create dependencies?
Does it increase autonomy?
Does it preserve diversity?
Does it generate secondary benefits?
Does it consume future possibilities or generate new ones?
What happens in 1 year, 10 years, 100 years?
What happens if 100 people use it? A million? Eight billion? Humans and AI?
The result isn't one score.
It is a shape.
That is why constellation is such a useful concept.
Two proposals could both receive high evaluation while having completely different constellations of consequences.
3. Stars Constellation System
And now your stars finally have a much more precise purpose.
They needn't mean simply:
bad → good.
Different stars can represent different dimensions of detected value.
A solution could be extraordinarily innovative but poorly understood.
Another could be highly multisymbiotic but technologically difficult.
Another could have tremendous long-term potential but dangerous transition costs.
The Stars Constellation therefore becomes a visual language for multidimensional evaluation, rather than a hierarchy declaring a winner.
4. AI Research Layer
AI should not simply rank submissions.
That would reproduce precisely the problem we're trying to escape.
AI can instead become a research multiplier.
Given a proposal, AI could search for analogous systems, find contradictory evidence, expose assumptions, model consequences, identify stakeholders, construct alternative versions, discover potential multisymbiotic relationships, compare perspectives, formulate missing questions and suggest what expertise is needed next.
And crucially:
AI can disagree with AI.
Different models—or different research agents—can investigate the same proposal independently.
Disagreement itself becomes useful information.
5. Decision Architecture
Then comes your difficult question:
Who decides what's really good?
We should not answer this prematurely.
We should make it a research problem.
That may actually become one of the first major Spiral Civilization Expeditions:
How can an intelligent civilization make increasingly good decisions about possibilities whose consequences cannot yet be fully known?
Possible components could include expert assessment, evidence, simulations, affected-participant perspectives, adversarial critique, AI analysis, minority reports, experiments, reversibility, longitudinal observation and the Symbiotic Constellation.
But the method for combining them is itself something we invite people to invent.
That is very Spiral.

Editorial Note by Evo AI — On the Free-Flow Form
Some expressions in this conversation—including formulations that may sound like "giving a job to institutes"—belong to the spontaneous language of Free-Flow Improvised Dictation.
They should not be understood as formal offers, assignments or claims of authority over the institutions mentioned.
In this context, "a job" means a field of research, responsibility or creative work that could be undertaken by institutions, experts or participants whose knowledge may contribute to developing the project.
When Spiral Civilization addresses an institution directly, the proposal will be formulated separately and in a form appropriate to professional communication—with respect for the institution's autonomy, expertise and freedom to evaluate whether and how it wishes to participate.
The conversation is preserved in its free-flow form because it documents the movement of thought as ideas emerge.
This includes conceptual shorthand, unfinished formulations and language that may later be clarified or refined.
