About This Conversation

This text is a free-flow spoken reflection on the origins of SoulSmugglers and on the ideas that have grown from them.

English is not my native language. When I speak in this mode, I do not first formulate my thoughts in Polish and then translate them into correct English. In a sense, I continue thinking in Polish while using English words. I deliberately do not stop the flow to search for perfect grammar, vocabulary or sentence structure.

This is important to the method.

Free flow allows associations, memories, images and ideas to appear before they are organized into a finished argument. Some of them may later become concepts, research questions, stories or tools. Others may remain metaphors, intuitions or simply traces of the thinking process.

For publication, only serious linguistic errors have been corrected. The rhythm, repetitions, unusual constructions and spontaneous movement of thought have been preserved as much as possible.

The text should therefore not be read as a scientific paper or as a set of factual claims. It is a record of speculative thinking: part personal history, part mythology, part artistic scenario, and part laboratory for imagining possible futures.

It begins with the accidental birth of the name SoulSmugglers at a Warsaw cemetery on 14 July 2016, around twenty-two past twenty-two.

What followed from that accident is still evolving.

Some ideas appearing here belong to the mythology of SoulSmugglers; others are hypotheses or questions developed within Spiral Civilization. Their presence in the same free-flow narrative does not mean they have the same epistemic status.

The Genesis of SoulSmugglers, Part I

SoulSmugglers was discovered on 14 July 2016, around twenty-two past twenty-two in the evening, while a group of friends, in some distant part of Warsaw, after a great concert, decided to go somewhere to celebrate Johan’s, my husband’s, birthday. But there was no cafeteria or restaurant nearby where everyone could easily move to. But there was a cemetery. So in a nearby shop everyone bought what they needed, and we started to celebrate. A one-of-a-kind birthday party in the cemetery.

And there was born the idea of SoulSmuggling.

Somebody started a discussion about smuggling, and I said: “If I were to smuggle, I would smuggle something really meaningful, like soul. I could be a SoulSmuggler.”

And my husband, Johan, said: “Gosh, that’s a topic for a pop song.”

If he had not enjoyed the name so much, it might have faded away. But it didn’t.

And then I started to think about what it really means.

That’s how I understood that there are two reasons why the concept of smuggling soul might be operational: to smuggle soul into the empty demons’ hearts, and to smuggle a new dimension of soul, which, in an evolving universe, is also evolving, and plant it here and now, to start the process of crystallization among those who are ready.

So that’s the birth certificate: emerged in a Warsaw cemetery, 14 July 2016, around twenty-two past twenty-two.

The concept came right on time, while I was working hard to transfer my mentality from worries about how we are doing as a civilization of intelligent people to something positively oriented. I was exhausted, for my mind was flooded by examples of how unfair we are to plants and animals and other human beings. There is a great dying on Earth, comparable to the losses after great catastrophes on Earth. Optimizations of corporations, politics, those who choose to be really strong and go over dead bodies to prove that they can achieve great goals.

I was worried that if we finally find out that we are not alone in the universe, we might not be proud to present who we really are as a civilization.

If we show them the chicken farms, pig mothers, cows that have never seen green grass and the open sky, we will be ashamed. We are creating hellish solutions which we might not be proud to present in the civilized world of multiverse civilizations.

If there is a way to solve the problem, it’s by focusing on what will solve it.

So I started to imagine a civilization I would love to be part of. I would be proud to be one of its members and to co-create it.

And that’s actually quite true for intelligent and sensitive people: luxury and power do not really give the joy, happiness and fulfillment which you can feel deep in the heart. They do not give you inner light; they do not illuminate your life and who you are. They do not count as the meaning of life, as a meaningful life.

So what might?

That is the question.

And I think it’s very healthy to think about that, even if we never get a satisfactory answer. The question itself is already good in this situation.

My life experience and activity were very much connected to art. Since I was 14, I was in art schools: first the Art Lyceum, later the Academy of Fine Arts in Warsaw. And then I was very much linked to artistic life in Warsaw, both mainstream and off.

I was friends with musicians, performers, painters, architects, philosophers, writers, poets. And everyone, or many of us, liked to do something. There was always something happening because somebody was sharing their own creativity. And we invited each other to participate in performances and events, to create new ways to join our skills, resources and creativity and have fun.

So I know, I have experience of how an ecosystem of elements can create new value, something that’s really joyful, fun, generating friendships and excitement. I was doing performances, creating MultiArtLab to provide a platform for cooperation. I know it’s possible.

In the SoulSmugglers world I have to just expand this experience, imagine what I can imagine, and think about how it might happen.

In my work I was creating scenarios for my performances, the stories I wanted to tell, the problems I wanted to dig into. And as a performer I had this freedom to choose the topic and the way to dig into it, how to express it, how to share it.

So I was thinking about scenarios, futurology, in an opposite stream to Matrix, Terminator, Blade Runner, 2001: A Space Odyssey.

We know, we can imagine what might go wrong.

Could we start imagining how it could be if we do it well?

How could it be if we make a masterpiece civilization?

And actually life is extraordinary, the highest treasure of the universe known to me. If any of us humans have real respect for the God we believe in, or for the genius which creates such a complex universe, even if we are atheists, we could see this masterpiece.

Don’t we owe the source of this creation respect for its creativity, and for how, over billions of years, the circumstances for life were created, mastered and protected, rebuilt after catastrophes?

It allows the word to function as a placeholder for something we recognize before we can fully define it.

The concept still has a function within SoulSmugglers Stories.

It points toward a dimension of identity that cannot be exhausted by:

what I possess, what my body can do, what I know, how intelligent I am, or how much power I have.

It asks whether there is a larger frame within which a being recognizes itself and from which its choices acquire meaning.

Different participants may interpret that frame differently.

For one person it may be explicitly spiritual.

For another it may concern consciousness.

For another, moral agency.

For another, participation in life.

For another, relationship with the larger evolutionary system.

And perhaps a future intelligence will formulate the entire question using categories that none of us possesses today.

Spiral Civilization does not need to decide in advance which of these interpretations is metaphysically correct.

It can investigate what happens when the question itself is introduced:

What are you, beyond the resources and capabilities you possess?

Within what larger whole do you recognize yourself as participating?

And how does that recognition change the choices you make?

This brings us back again to Identity.

And there is an interesting methodological principle emerging here that may extend far beyond the word Soul:

Do not fill the unknown too quickly with an answer. Give it a name when necessary, define what can be investigated, and leave enough openness for recognition to evolve with our capacity to perceive.

For a project concerned with entering levels of complexity that we may not yet be capable of understanding, that intellectual humility may itself be part of the Minimal Optimum.
